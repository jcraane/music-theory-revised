# 08: Borrowed chords and modal mixture

**Status:** outlined
**Builds on:** 02, 07

## Goal
Use chords borrowed from the parallel key to shift emotion on purpose.

## Key concepts
- **Parallel key:** same tonic, other mode (C major and C minor).
- **Modal mixture:** borrowing chords from the parallel key.
- **Common borrowed chords in major:** iv, bVI, bVII, bIII.
- **Picardy third:** ending a minor-key piece on a major I.
- **Borrowing in minor:** major IV (from Dorian) and major I.

## Sections
1. **Parallel keys:** C major and C minor side by side; which notes differ.
2. **The minor iv:** the bittersweet move from lesson 01's experiment, now explained.
3. **bVI and bVII:** heroic and epic; bVI–bVII–I as a triumphant ending.
4. **bIII:** a rock and film color.
5. **The major lift in minor:** the Picardy third and the "sunrise" moment.
6. **Borrowing in minor keys:** major IV and I for brightness.

## Main experiment
Swap-a-chord on a loop: replace any chord with a borrowed option and see an emotional
label and the changed notes highlighted.

## Quiz ideas
Spot the borrowed chord by ear; name the borrowed chord that contains a given note.

## Components and theory
- **New:** swap-a-chord component; parallel key view.
- **Theory:** `parallelKey(key)`, `borrowedChords(tonic, fromMode)`, `isDiatonic(chord, key)`.

## Open questions
- Emotional labels are subjective: phrase them as "often heard as" rather than facts.
