# 03: Cadences: how phrases end

**Status:** outlined
**Builds on:** 02

## Goal
Recognize the main cadence types by ear and use them to shape the ends of phrases.

## Key concepts
- **Phrase:** a musical sentence, usually 4 or 8 bars.
- **Cadence:** the chord move that ends a phrase.
- **Authentic cadence (V–I):** a full stop.
- **Plagal cadence (IV–I):** a softer, "amen" ending.
- **Half cadence (ends on V):** a comma or question mark.
- **Deceptive cadence (V–vi):** a surprise; the phrase continues.
- **Aeolian cadence (bVI–bVII–i):** the typical minor-key ending in rock, film and trance.

## Sections
1. **Phrases as sentences:** punctuation analogy; an 8-bar phrase with a simple top-line
   melody so the ending feels real.
2. **Authentic:** V–I, the strongest ending.
3. **Plagal:** IV–I, gentler and warmer.
4. **Half cadence:** ending on V leaves a question hanging; often answered by the next phrase.
5. **Deceptive:** V–vi, the ear expects home and gets a stand-in.
6. **Cadences in minor:** v–i versus V–i, and the Aeolian cadence VI–VII–i.

## Main experiment
One 8-bar phrase with four interchangeable endings. The user picks the ending and hears
how the whole phrase changes meaning.

## Quiz ideas
Ear: identify the cadence type at the end of a phrase.

## Components and theory
- **New:** phrase player (chords plus a simple generated top line).
- **Theory:** `cadenceType(chordA, chordB, key)`.

## Open questions
- How much melody is needed for phrases to feel like phrases? A simple top voice
  derived from the voicing may be enough.
