# 10: Secondary dominants

**Status:** outlined
**Builds on:** 02, 03, 06

## Goal
Use secondary dominants to briefly point towards chords other than the tonic, adding
momentum to progressions.

## Key concepts
- **Tonicization:** briefly treating a chord other than I as a temporary home.
- **Secondary dominant (V/x):** the dominant of a chord in the key, e.g. V/V (D in C).
- **Secondary leading tone:** the out-of-key note, a half step below the target root.
- **Tonicizable chords:** any major or minor diatonic chord (not vii°).
- **Tonicization versus modulation:** a brief visit versus actually moving home.

## Sections
1. **The pull of V, recap:** V–I and V7–I.
2. **Borrowing that pull:** D–G in C (V/V–V).
3. **V/ii and V/vi:** the A major and E major from lesson 01's experiment, now explained.
4. **Chains:** E7–A7–D7–G7–C, falling fifths on the circle.
5. **Spotting them:** the out-of-key note is always a leading tone to the next root.

## Main experiment
Insert a secondary dominant before each chord of a simple loop and hear the momentum grow.

## Quiz ideas
What is V/x in a given key; ear: diatonic or secondary dominant.

## Components and theory
- **New:** "insert secondary dominant" action in the loop builder; reuse the circle of fifths.
- **Theory:** `secondaryDominant(target, key)`, `isSecondaryDominant(chord, next, key)`.
