# 04: Root motion and the circle of fifths

**Status:** outlined
**Builds on:** 01, 02

## Goal
See and hear why root moves by fifths feel strong, by thirds soft, and by steps like
climbing or sinking, using the circle of fifths as a map.

## Key concepts
- **Circle of fifths:** the 12 keys arranged so neighbors are a fifth apart and differ by one note.
- **Closely related keys:** neighbors on the circle, plus their relative minors.
- **Root motion:** the interval between the roots of consecutive chords.
- **Common tones:** notes two chords share (5th apart: one; 3rd apart: two; step: none).

## Sections
1. **The circle:** click to play keys; watch sharps and flats accumulate around it.
2. **A key's chords are neighbors:** the diatonic chord roots form one arc of the circle
   (F–C–G–D–A–E–B in C major).
3. **Falling fifths:** vi–ii–V–I as a chain of strong moves, drawn as a path.
4. **Moves by thirds:** I–vi, I–iii; two shared notes make them smooth and colorful.
5. **Moves by step:** IV–V, bVI–bVII–I; climbing energy, no shared notes.
6. **Progressions as paths:** any loop drawn on the circle; its shape tells you its character.

## Main experiment
Reroute a progression: keep the start and end chord, change the path in between
(fifths versus thirds versus steps) and compare the feel.

## Quiz ideas
Which chord is a fifth below X; classify a root move; how many common tones.

## Components and theory
- **New:** circle of fifths component with path drawing.
- **Theory:** `circleOrder()`, `rootMotion(a, b)`, `commonTones(a, b)`, `keySignature(key)`.
